# DatanomyUK-Remote-Work-Survey-Sample-Dataset---PBI

## Key KPIs for Remote Working Survey
## Demographics & Workforce Profile
1.	Respondent Count (Total Responses)
2.	Gender Distribution
3.	Age Group Distribution (based on birth year)
4.	Industry & Occupation Breakdown
5.	Organisation Size Distribution
6.	Manager vs. Non-Manager Ratio
7.	Metro vs. Regional Breakdown

## Remote Work Adoption (Past Year & Last 3 Months)
8.	Average % Time Spent Remote Working
9.	Preferred vs. Actual Remote Work Hours (Gap Analysis)
10.	% Respondents Who Prefer More Remote Work
11.	Change in Remote Working % – Year vs. 3 Months

## Perception & Satisfaction Indicators
12.	% Agree/Strongly Agree – My organisation encouraged remote work
13.	% Agree/Strongly Agree – Collaboration was easy remotely
14.	% Recommend Remote Working to Others
15.	% Who Felt Organisation Was Prepared for Remote Work
(Each of these should be broken into “Past Year” and “Last 3 Months” for trend comparison.)

## Productivity & Time Use
16.	Average Reported Productivity When Remote (vs. In-office)
17.	Time Spent Comparison:
o	Commuting (Remote vs. In-office)
o	Working Hours (Remote vs. In-office)
o	Personal/Family Time
o	Domestic Responsibilities

## Barriers to Remote Work
18.	Top 3 Most Significant Barriers (Ranked/Filtered)
19.	Top 3 Least Significant Barriers
20.	Barrier Types by Role, Industry, or Location

## Future Expectations Post-COVID
21.	% Who Want to Continue Remote Work
22.	% Who Expect Employer to Support Remote Work
23.	% Expecting More Choice in Remote vs. On-site

## Best/Worst Aspects of Remote Work (Categorical Analysis)
24.	Top-Ranked Best Aspects (e.g., Work-life balance, Expenses)
25.	Top-Ranked Worst Aspects (e.g., Isolation, Career growth)

# Power BI Report Page Suggestions
## Page 1 – Executive Summary Dashboard
•	Key KPIs: Total responses, gender/age distribution, avg. remote hours, productivity score
•	High-level charts comparing past year vs. last 3 months
## Page 2 – Remote Work Trends
•	Line/Bar charts: % time spent remotely (year, 3 months)
•	Preferred vs. actual working time
•	Organisation preparedness perception
## Page 3 – Demographics
•	Filters by gender, industry, age group, region
•	Role-based or industry-specific breakdowns
## Page 4 – Barriers Analysis
•	Treemap or stacked bar of most/least significant barriers
•	Filters by role, org size, metro/regional
## Page 5 – Work-Life and Productivity
•	Comparison charts of daily activities
•	Productivity perception (numeric or index-based)
## Page 6 – Future Outlook
•	Preferences post-COVID
•	Expected employer support changes

## Note
In addition to the listed KPIs, closely review the dataset to identify hidden patterns or emerging trends that could be valuable for HR leadership, workplace strategy teams, or executive decision-makers. Examples may include:
•	Variations in remote work preference or satisfaction across age groups, gender, or regions (e.g., younger employees preferring more in-office time).
•	Discrepancies in perceived barriers to remote work between managers and non-managers.
•	Emerging trends in productivity perception vs. actual hours worked.
•	Notable regional or industry-specific differences in employer support or preparedness for remote work.
•	Potential disconnects between actual remote work and preferred remote work across different job roles or organisation sizes.
